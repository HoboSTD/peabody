These four tiles are a snapshot of the single most recent reading only. The sections below explain what each measurement is and why it's used.

Water quality

Each chart tracks one property of the water, measured every 15 minutes at all four sites by automatic sensors.

Specific conductivity

Specific conductivity measures how easily electricity passes through the water, which depends on how much dissolved mineral and salt content it carries — purer water conducts less, water carrying more dissolved solids conducts more. It's measured in microsiemens per centimetre (µS/cm) and is one of the simplest ways to tell whether something has added dissolved material to the water, since it responds quickly with no lab test needed. Comparing Downstream to Upstream, rather than looking at Downstream alone, matters because conductivity naturally rises and falls with rainfall and the seasons at both sites together — a gap that opens up between the two lines, beyond how they normally relate, is the signal worth attention.

Turbidity

Turbidity measures how cloudy or murky the water looks, caused by fine particles — sediment, silt, organic matter — suspended in it, measured in NTU (nephelometric turbidity units; higher means cloudier). Rain and natural runoff stir up sediment too, so turbidity is naturally spiky rather than steady at every site: a mostly-flat line with occasional sharp spikes is the expected shape, not a fault.

pH

pH measures how acidic or alkaline water is, on a scale from 0 (very acidic) to 14 (very alkaline); 7 is neutral, and most natural fresh water sits a little either side of that. The shaded band (6.5–8.5) is the specific range the mine's EPA discharge licence requires its treated wastewater to stay within before release — a regulatory limit, not a natural law — so a reading outside the band at a discharge point (LDP7/LDP8) means that particular licence condition wasn't met at that moment.

Discharge flow

"Discharge" is treated water the mine releases into the creek from two specific, licensed points — LDP7 (the water treatment plant) and LDP8 (Turkeys Nest). This chart shows litres released per 15 minutes, so its timing can be compared against the conductivity chart above. Most of the time there's nothing to show here: LDP7 only reports a reading at all while it's actively discharging, which the full history shows happening on only a handful of days a year, and LDP8's flow reads as zero almost all the time too. An empty chart below means no discharge was recorded in this window, not a missing-data problem.

Flagged periods

A "flagged period" is a stretch of time where Downstream's specific conductivity was notably higher than Upstream's, relative to how those two sites normally compare — generated automatically by checking that ratio against fixed thresholds, not by a person reviewing the data. Confidence reflects how much supporting evidence there is beyond the conductivity ratio alone:

  • High — the ratio was extreme, and a turbidity spike or active discharge was recorded at the same time.
  • Medium — either the ratio alone was extreme, or a smaller rise coincided with a turbidity spike or discharge.
  • Low — the ratio was somewhat elevated, with no other supporting signal.

"Corroborated by" lists which supporting signal(s), if any, lined up with the period.

Chronic trend

The conductivity ratio above moves around a lot day to day. This chart separates that short-term noise from any longer-run change: the thin line is the median ratio for each individual day, and the bold line smooths that into a rolling 90-day median. A sustained shift in the bold line — rather than a single day's spike — is what would indicate the underlying relationship between the two sites is changing over time.

Data health

Not every 15-minute interval has a usable reading at every site — equipment occasionally drops out, and some sites are designed to report data only some of the time. This table shows, per site, what share of expected readings are present and valid, and lists any stretches of 4 hours or more with no record at all. A low number isn't automatically a problem: LDP7 Water Treatment Plant is expected to score low here, because that point only takes a reading at all while it's actively discharging, which happens on only a handful of days a year — the rest of the time there's simply nothing for it to record.
